Built on plain files

Notes are stored as .md files with YAML frontmatter — the same structure Obsidian vaults use. Nothing proprietary, nothing to migrate later. Open your notes in any text editor, on any device, at any time.

Private by default

Your notes stay on this device until you explicitly turn on a sync destination. There's no IOTHS account and no developer-operated sign-in. Usage analytics are off unless you turn them on, and even then they never include note content, titles, or tags. Nothing is sold. Nothing trains an AI model. Read the full Privacy Policy.

  • Files folder storage, through any Files-compatible provider such as an Obsidian vault, Box, Dropbox, or Microsoft OneDrive

Capture, editing, reading, sharing, PDF conversion, tagging, task tracking, archive, and trash recovery are free, forever.

Why this exists

I built IOTHS for myself, first. I'd never found a notes app that actually fit — not functionally, not in how it felt to use — so I made the one I wanted. That's where it started: not to monetize it, not to be seen. Just to have it.

The icon is a small flirtation with complexity theory — a note dissolving into scattered blocks, the way Conway's Game of Life turns a handful of simple rules into patterns nobody predicted. Notes, todos, and tasks feel orderly until they don't. The same structure that organizes a day can just as easily spill into something stranger than intended.
