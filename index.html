A small idea about emergence

Start with the smallest true thing.

Knowledge work rarely arrives as a neat plan. It starts as an observation, a question, or a half-formed thought. In IOTHS, that thought can stay a note until a few checkboxes reveal work worth doing.

The app does not pretend to predict what happens next. It gives the work somewhere calm to change shape: a note becomes a checklist, a checklist becomes active work, and the board shows the pattern that is already there.

  1. 01Notice Write the first useful sentence.
  2. 02Explore Add a checklist when there are next steps.
  3. 03Commit Mark one note in progress when it matters now.
  4. 04See Let the task view and board make the work visible.

Generation 0
Every square plays a tiny survival game: living cells survive with two or three neighbours, and empty squares are born with three. This is a playful reference, not a model of your work.

No black box

Built on plain files

Notes are stored as .md files with YAML frontmatter — the same structure Obsidian vaults use. Nothing proprietary, nothing to migrate later. Open your notes in any text editor, on any device, at any time.

Private by default

Your notes stay on this device until you explicitly turn on a sync destination. There's no IOTHS account and no developer-operated sign-in. Usage analytics are off unless you turn them on, and even then they never include note content, titles, or tags. Nothing is sold. Nothing trains an AI model. Read the full Privacy Policy.

Why this exists

I built IOTHS for myself, first. I'd never found a notes app that actually fit — not functionally, not in how it felt to use — so I made the one I wanted. That's where it started: not to monetize it, not to be seen. Just to have it.

The icon is a small nod to Conway's Game of Life and complexity theory: simple local rules, surprising patterns. It is a familiar way to think about emergence in complex adaptive systems, not a claim that IOTHS models them. IOTHS is simply a place where a thought can stay small until the work asks for more structure.
